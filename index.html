the problem

Music was built for ears.

Over 430 million people worldwide are deaf or hard-of-hearing, and most music apps weren't made with them in mind. No captions, no visualization, no way in. Audra rebuilds the listening experience around sight and touch instead of sound.

see

Frequency Landscape

A live visualizer renders 64 frequency bins from a real-time FFT pipeline, redrawn 60 times a second. It's the shape of the music, made visible.

feel

Rhythm Grid + Haptics

Beat-synced Core Haptics pulses let you feel the rhythm in your hand, mapped directly to the same audio analysis driving the visuals.

know

Emotion Fingerprint

A five-dimensional emotional signature locks in after five seconds of listening. It gives a shape and color to how a song feels, not just what it sounds like.

tune

Personal Hearing Profiles

Per-band frequency boost sliders let each listener shape the mix around their own hearing range, not a generic default.

try it yourself

This is a real pipeline, live in your browser.

This isn't a canned animation. When you press play, your browser generates a short musical phrase on the spot and runs it through a real AnalyserNode FFT, the same kind of analysis Audra's AudioEngine.swift uses to process live audio on iOS. The bars below are reacting to actual frequency data pulled from the sound as it plays, the rhythm grid is lighting up based on real low-end energy, and the radar chart is calculated fresh from the spectrum every frame. Nothing here is pre-rendered.
